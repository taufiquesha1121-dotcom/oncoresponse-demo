## RQ1 — Cross-cohort robustness

**The elastic net lost almost nothing across cohorts: 0.758 → 0.750 (−0.008).**

That is the headline, and it is genuinely surprising — but read it next to the
in-sample number, because the two together are the actual story:

```
elastic net, in-sample on discovery   0.892
elastic net, nested CV on discovery   0.758    <- optimism costs 0.134
elastic net, validation, single touch 0.750    <- cohort change costs 0.008
```

**The gap that matters is not between cohorts — it is between "what the model
scores on its own training data" and "what it scores on data it has never
seen."** Roughly 0.13 AUC of the 0.89 headline is an artefact of evaluating on
training data. Cross-cohort transfer is, by comparison, nearly free. A report
that quoted 0.892 would have been wrong by a factor of five more than one that
quoted the cohort-comparison gap alone.

**The random forest is the cautionary tale.** It hits ROC-AUC 1.000 in-sample —
it memorised the 306 discovery patients perfectly — and then drops to 0.702 in
validation, *below* the linear model. On a fixed hyperparameter budget the
non-linear model is strictly worse, and its in-sample score is a perfect
predictor of how badly it will fail out of cohort.

### At the default 0.5 threshold

| Model | Cohort | Sens. | Spec. | PPV | NPV | Brier |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| elastic net | discovery (in-sample) | 0.070 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 0.825 | 0.110 |
| elastic net | **validation** | **0.071** | **0.986** | 0.600 | 0.780 | **0.155** |
| random forest | discovery (in-sample) | 0.807 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 0.958 | 0.047 |
| random forest | **validation** | **0.000** | 1.000 | — | 0.769 | 0.162 |

Sensitivity of 0.07 in validation is not a bug — it is the model refusing to
call anyone a responder. Because only 23% of validation patients are pCR and
the model is heavily regularised toward the majority class, almost no score
clears 0.5 (see the score-distribution panel in `evaluation.png`). **Anyone
using a 0.5 cut-off on this model in a clinic would treat 93% of true
responders as non-responders.**

At the Youden threshold selected on discovery and frozen (0.2586), the same
model gets **sensitivity 0.524 / specificity 0.793** in validation — a far more
usable operating point. This is exactly why the discovery-selected threshold is
reported alongside the default, and why tuning it on validation would have been
cheating.

## RQ2 — Feature stability

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Mean pairwise Jaccard across bootstrap selected sets | **0.265** (SD 0.064) |
| Genes selected in ≥60% of resamples | **11** of 9825 |
| …of those, also BH-FDR < 0.05 | **11** |
| Genes with BH-FDR < 0.05 (any stability) | 2043 |

**Jaccard 0.265 means two bootstrap resamples of the same cohort, same model,
same hyperparameters, agree on roughly a quarter of their gene list.** With
p ≫ n that is the expected result, not a malfunction — and it is the most
useful number in the whole audit. Any "signature" reported from a single
fit on this data is substantially a property of *which patients happened to be
enrolled*, not a stable biological feature set.

Note the asymmetry that makes this reportable rather than merely negative:
**2043 genes are statistically associated with response, but only 11 are
reliably re-selected.** Statistical significance and selection stability are
different properties, and only the 11 satisfy both. Those 11 are:

`METRN, ATG14, GMPS, DEK, MELK, CDKN2A, E2F3, ILF2, NFIB, DSC2, TMSB15B`

(MELK, CDKN2A, E2F3, GMPS, DEK, ILF2 are proliferation/cell-cycle genes — a
coherent group, and consistent with proliferation being a known component of
response signatures in this literature. Stated as a coherence observation, not
a claim of mechanism; nothing here tests function.)

## RQ3 — Calibration

Brier score degrades 0.110 → 0.155 from discovery to validation. The
calibration panel in `evaluation.png` shows why: predicted probabilities span
roughly 0.10–0.41 while observed pCR rates in the top bin reach 0.48.

Per-bin gaps for the elastic net on validation
(`calibration_elasticnet_validation.csv`):

| Bin | n | mean predicted | observed | gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 37 | 0.105 | 0.027 | −0.078 |
| 2 | 36 | 0.139 | 0.111 | −0.028 |
| 3 | 36 | 0.174 | **0.306** | **+0.131** |
| 4 | 36 | 0.249 | 0.222 | −0.026 |
| 5 | 37 | 0.408 | 0.486 | +0.078 |

The model **under-calls risk in the middle of its range** (bin 3 under-predicts
by 0.13) and under-predicts the top bin as well. Ranking is decent; the
probabilities themselves should not be read as risks. This is the concrete
demonstration of why RQ3 is a separate question from RQ1 — a model can hold
ROC-AUC 0.75 while being badly calibrated, and only the calibration curve shows
it.

A contributing cause is visible in the data itself: the pCR base rate shifts
18.6% → 23.1% between cohorts. The model was fitted under the lower prevalence
and is applied under the higher one. No prevalence correction was applied, and
none should be — correcting requires knowing the validation labels in advance,
which is precisely the leak this project exists to avoid.

## RQ4 — Where it breaks

Ranked by how much each explains the observed behaviour:

1. **In-sample evaluation.** Largest single distortion (0.892 vs 0.758). Not a
   pipeline defect — the point is that it is invisible unless you run nested CV.
2. **Non-regularised model class.** RF memorised discovery (AUC 1.000) and
   generalised worst. p ≫ n punishes anything that can interpolate.
3. **Prevalence shift**, 18.6% → 23.1%, driving the miscalibration.
4. **Selection instability.** Jaccard 0.265 caps how much any single fitted
   model can be trusted as a "signature."

**Deliberately not fixed:** no ComBat/batch correction between the two centres
(UCL vs USO). Applying it would erase the very generalisation gap the audit
measures, and estimating the batch parameters would require the validation
cohort — a leak. The gap is the result, not a defect to be engineered away.

---

## What a reader should take away

1. Quote **0.758** (nested CV), not 0.892. The difference is entirely
   self-deception and has nothing to do with the cohorts.
2. The elastic net **did** transport (0.758 → 0.750). With strong regularisation
   at p ≫ n, a simple linear model is genuinely robust here.
3. But the **features are not stable** (Jaccard 0.265), so the model's success
   should not be read as "we have found the genes that matter."
4. The probabilities are **not calibrated** and must not be used as risks.
